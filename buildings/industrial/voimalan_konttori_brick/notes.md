# voimalan_konttori_brick

## Style brief

The office and control building of a small Nordic town's coal-fired heat and
power plant (*voimalan konttori*: the power plant's office), 1950s-60s. It
is one of the plant's separate buildings and holds its main entrance:
visitors and staff come in here, and the plant is run from the control room
on its ground floor. A generic composite of the type, not a portrait of any
one building.

- **Two storeys, 14 x 9 m**, long side to the front, red facing brick on a
  cast concrete plinth, in restrained post-war functionalism: a light cast
  band at the first floor right round the building, plain brick otherwise.
- **A flat roof** behind a low 0.3125 m brick parapet with a slim galvanised
  sheet coping, drained inside through two outlets in the deck: the box of
  an institutional or industrial building, not a house or a school.
- **Windows in bands of white steel lights.** Upstairs one continuous ribbon
  along each long side, 12 m of 1.5 m lights, brick only at its ends, its
  head at the ceiling tight under the roof edge, a slim transom line across
  it under a fixed upper light, a side-hung vent sash in every other light
  below the transom. Downstairs the control room's
  taller band of four lights fills the east half of front and rear, a row
  of top-hung vents across its head; the offices' two-light windows speak
  the same language smaller. One two-light window to each storey on the
  ends.
- **The main entrance** on the front, one bay west of the middle: a pair of
  glazed teak leaves under a transom light in a cast concrete surround,
  under a slab canopy cantilevered 2.125 m from the first-floor slab with
  no posts, its deep fascia turned up over the slab, and a cast concrete
  fin under its west edge, away from the control room, taking the steps'
  west cheek; up three steps with a handrail. The plant clock hangs on the
  brick beside it, between the entrance and the control room.
- **The staff door** to the boiler house: a steel door on the rear, up
  three steps with a handrail, a cast bulkhead lamp over it (painted, not
  lit).
- **Roof:** a galvanised ventilation housing, the control room's radio
  mast (a galvanised tube with a dipole across its head, a whip on top and
  two stays), two plain vent pipes. The housing is serviced from the roof,
  so a caged wall ladder climbs the west end next to its rear corner.

No signage, lettering or company marks anywhere on it: the plant's hall
carries the name.

## Category and secondary use

`buildings/industrial/`, as part of a heat and power plant. Secondary use:
**offices** (upper floor and the ground floor's west end) and the plant's
**control room** (ground floor, east half).

**None: a flat roof behind a parapet.** The galvanised coping oversails the

## Construction

Closed solids unioned with Blender's exact boolean (`asset_build.union`):

1. **the shell**: four wall regions walked counter-clockwise, ground to
   7.25 m, each opening built into its region as reveals round a closing
   plane (cut tiers, `LOD0`-`LOD2`) or flat quads (the flush tier, `LOD3`),
   the ring capped by a
   floor on the ground and, at the top, the parapet's 0.25 m thick top, its
   inner faces down to the deck, and the deck at 7.0 m, so it is a solid of
   the building's whole volume with the roof sunk in it;
2. **the coping**: one closed ring 0.125 m deep, 7.1875-7.3125 m,
   oversailing both faces of the parapet by 0.0625 m; the parapet's top
   runs 0.0625 m up into it;
3. **the canopy**: a 0.375 m slab, 3.5-3.875 m, sunk 0.1 m into the front
   wall, its soffit level with the painted slab band's underside, and **the
   fin**, a 0.2 x 1.2 m box from the ground 0.05 m up into the slab, its
   back sunk 0.1 m into the wall and its east face 5 cm into the steps'
   west cheek;
4. **two flights of steps**, profiles extruded across the doors, their backs
   sunk 0.05 m into the wall below each threshold;
5. **the clock**, a twelve-sided drum sunk 0.05 m into the front wall, and
   **the lamp**, a box sunk 0.05 m into the rear wall;
6. **the roof plant**: the ventilation housing (1.5 x 1.5 x 0.94 m), the
   mast (below), two vent pipes, each sunk 0.1 m into the deck.

The union trims everything into the shell and leaves the boundary of one
empty volume. A 0.1 mm weld follows; at `LOD2` and `LOD3` a limited dissolve
(0.001 rad, UV seams kept) recovers coplanar boolean subdivisions. Then
**the lean kit** goes in on every tier, appended rather than unioned
(`roof_safety.append(..., check=True)`, which fails the build if any piece
touches the building or another piece): the wall ladder and its cage
(`roof_safety.WallLadder(..., lean=True)`, `Cage`) and the two handrails
(`handrails.Flight(..., lean=True)`). The plinth is painted flush with the
brick, so nothing stands proud behind the ladder's brackets and the ladder
takes no `behind`.

Every number the mesh and the atlas share is in `facade_atlas.py`: the plan
(HX = 7, HY = 4.5), the floor, slab band, deck, coping, sill, leaf,
entrance and canopy heights, the parapet's thickness (PIN = 0.25), each
window band's light width and height, and every opening's size and reveal.
`build.py` keeps the elevations' opening lists, the canopy, fin, step,
rail, clock, lamp, plant and ladder positions, and `check_layout()`, which
fails the build if:

- an opening (the entrance with its surround) comes within 0.4 m of a corner
  or 0.3 m of another opening, within 0.4 m of the deck, or into the slab
  band;
- the ribbon's or the control band's mullions leave 1.2-1.5 m, the
  ribbon's head is not at a 2.6-2.8 m ceiling, more than 0.75 m of brick
  stands over it, its fixed upper light leaves a slim 0.4-0.6 m, or the
  control band is not taller than the offices' windows;
- a leaf leaves 2.0-2.4 m, the entrance has no transom light, any sill
  leaves desk height, either storey leaves 3.0-3.3 m, or the parapet leaves
  0.3-0.5 m over the deck;
- the canopy leaves the surround, is not wider than it, leaves 2.0-2.2 m
  out, comes within 0.25 m of a window or does not cover the landing; the
  fin runs into the surround, misses the steps' west cheek, or falls short
  of the landing's edge or outruns the slab;
- the clock comes within 0.3 m of an opening or of the canopy's height, or
  the lamp runs into the slab band;
- the entrance steps are narrower than the surround, or a riser exceeds
  0.18 m;
- a handrail crowds its door or its foot post leaves its tread
  (`handrails.problems()`);
- a piece of roof plant (the mast's stays' feet included) comes within
  0.4 m of the parapet, 0.3 m of another or 0.4 m of an outlet;
- the ladder or its cage crowds a corner or a window of the west end
  (`roof_safety.problems()`).

### The parapet and the deck

The parapet is the walls' brick carried 0.25 m above the deck, 0.3125 m to
the coping's top: lowered from 0.375 m so the slim coping sits close over
the ribbon's head. Its inner face samples the upstand band (the deck's felt dressed up over a cant to a
termination bar under the coping), its top is buried in the coping. The
coping's ring carries a face, a top and a drip underside; it oversails the
deck side by the same 0.0625 m. The deck is level in the mesh; its falls to
the outlets are in the paint.

### Window bands

A band of n lights is one opening n light widths long, with one set of
reveals. Its closing plane is split at every mullion into n quads, each
mapped onto its own light's region (`facade_atlas.light_kind`): every light
carries half a mullion at each side, so two neighbours make a whole one and
the band's ends get a frame of the same width. The band's outline carries
the split points along its sill and head, so the sill and head reveals and
the wall around meet the lights on shared vertices. Every ribbon light
carries a painted 6 cm transom bar under a fixed 0.5 m upper light, the
bars meeting across the mullions in one line. Below it the ribbon
alternates a fixed light with one carrying a 0.44 m side-hung vent; its
two-light end windows are two fixed lights, since a vent between two lights
read as a house's casement. The control band and the offices' windows carry a
0.375 m row of top-hung vents across every light, one line along the
ground storey.

### The entrance

The entrance region in the atlas holds the cast surround and the doors
together. At `LOD0`-`LOD2` the surround is a U of concrete on the wall face
round the opening, the wall's hole is the surround's outline with the
opening's foot corners on it, and the reveals are concrete, the threshold
a grey sill. At `LOD3` the whole surround is one quad. The canopy's
underside cuts the surround 6 cm below its top, so the two read as one
piece, and the canopy's soffit and the lower 0.25 m of its fascia line up
with the painted first-floor band, so it reads as the slab carried out over
the door, its edge turned up 0.125 m above the band into a deeper fascia.

### The canopy and its fin

The canopy is cantilevered, with no posts: its two slim posts read
spindly, and one stood in front of the offices' window in the catalog
view. Three forms were rendered from the catalog camera: a fin the
canopy's whole 2.125 m depth (it closed the entrance into a box and hid the
offices' window), a 2.75 m canopy on a round steel column at its outer
east corner and a fin (the column then stood in front of the doors), and
the kept one: a 2.125 m cantilever and a fin 1.2 m deep, to the landing's
edge, under the slab's west edge, away from the control room's band. The
fin frames the entrance as a wing wall, takes the steps' west cheek, and
leaves the offices' window clear in the catalog view; the slab runs on
0.9 m past it. The handrail is on the flight's east side, so the fin does
not crowd it.

### The clock and the lamp

The plant clock, a 0.625 m dial in a dark steel bezel on a 6 cm drum, hangs
on the brick between the entrance's surround and the control room's band,
its centre at 2.5625 m: a mark of a works office the yard reads its time
from. The staff door's cast bulkhead lamp, 0.25 x 0.3125 m and 0.14 m
deep, its frosted glass to the front and underneath, sits 0.1875 m over the
door's head. It is painted, not emissive: no lamp is luminous (Night
lighting).

### Steps and handrails

Three risers of 0.156 m to a landing 1.2 m (front) or 1.0 m (rear) deep,
two 0.3 m treads below it. Three risers, so a handrail on each flight, on
the right going up (`scripts/handrails.py`, lean, the cheapest form the
script builds), kept one tier past its `MODES`, on `LOD0`-`LOD2`: dropped at
`LOD2` they visibly went from the entrance flight at the consumer's switch. The rails take the coping's galvanised colour (`PIPE_STRIP`,
the middle rows of the coping's top). The steps stay on every tier.

### Roof plant and the ladder

The ventilation housing over the stair is serviced from the roof, so the
deck needs a fixed route up (CLAUDE.md): a wall ladder only, the deck being
level. At the back, but the rear wall's ribbon leaves no run clear of
openings, so it climbs the blank part of the west end, 1.2 m from the rear
corner, out of the catalog view. It runs from the ground to 1.0 m above the
coping, **8.3125 m**, over `roof_safety.CAGE_LADDER` (8 m) even with the
lowered parapet, so it carries the script's cage, hoops from 2.5 m; it is
not shortened under the 1 m handhold to lose the cage. It is the lean kit:
the same rails, rungs, brackets, hoops and straps as separate solids that
touch nothing, so nothing is cut into the rails or the wall. The kit samples
`PIPE_STRIP` like the rails. `roof_safety.MODES`, `CAGE_MODES` and
`LEAN_FORMS` unchanged: every rung and hoop at `LOD0`, thinning through
`LOD2`; `LOD3` keeps the bare rails and the cage's back strap
(`solids(extents=True)`), which set the plan's west extent. The mast and
vents are not serviced items.

### The radio mast

The control room's radio mast, at (3.0, 2.5) over the control room: a
0.1 m galvanised tube 2.5 m over the deck, a 1.3 m dipole bar across its
head 0.2 m under the top, a 2.5 cm whip 0.75 m over the tube, and two
2.5 cm stays from 1.6 m up the tube to the deck 1.2 m out on its yard side,
all closed solids in the union. It replaces a 6 cm pole with a small
directional aerial that read as thin clutter, and was kept over removing it
because, rendered, the box's roofline with only the housing and the vents
read as any office; the mast says control room. The tube and the whip set
the building's height, so they stay on every tier (the tube eight, six,
four and four sided); the dipole and the stays to `LOD2` (the stays' two
diagonals visibly went at the consumer's `LOD2` switch when they stopped at
`LOD1`).

### Rainwater

Internal drainage: two outlets painted in the deck at x = -3 and 3, y = -1.5,
with ponding stains round them. With no gutter there is no downpipe
(CLAUDE.md); an outlet piped down the outside was judged to add nothing the
box needs.

## Export

`.glb` + external PNGs through `scripts/asset_build.py`:
`export_format='GLB'`, `use_selection=True`, `export_yup=True`,
`export_apply=True`, `export_keep_originals=True`, `export_cameras=False`,
`export_lights=False`. Emission is exported lit and the factor rewritten to
zero. `strip_embedded_images()` removes the placeholder `bufferView`s.

Checked by the validator on all four tiers:

- one mesh, one material slot;
- `baseColorTexture` → `voimalan_konttori_brick_albedo_red_brick.png`;
- `metallicRoughnessTexture` and `occlusionTexture` → `..._orm.png`;
- `normalTexture` → `..._normal.png`, scale 1, no tangents;
- `emissiveTexture` → `..._emission.png`, `emissiveFactor [0, 0, 0]`;
- every image a `uri`, no `bufferView`.

Pivot: ground level, centre of `LOD0`'s plan bounds, applied to every tier.
Front faces `-Y` in Blender, `+Z` in the exported glTF frame.

The facts sidecar `export/voimalan_konttori_brick.model.json` lists:

- **footprint:** the 14 x 9 m outer wall faces; the coping's oversail, the
  canopy and its fin, the clock, the lamp, the steps and rails and the
  caged ladder stand outside it;
- **storeys:** two, floors 0.5 m and 3.75 m, 126 m2 each (the upper one
  under the flat roof, 3.25 m to the deck);
- **dwellings:** 0;
- **doors:** `entrance` (main, kind entrance, front, the pair 1.5 m clear)
  and `staff` (kind service, rear).
